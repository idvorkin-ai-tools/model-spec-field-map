OpenAI's Model Spec, mapped

The Model Spec is OpenAI's public statement of how its GPT models should behave: who they take orders from, what they will never do, how honest they must be, and how they should treat the people they work for. The 29 October 2026 version reorganises it around five values and spells out an authority ladder for every rule. This site explains it part by part, then sets it beside Claude's 2026 constitution.

Is this OpenAI's version of Claude's constitution? Mostly, yes. Both documents say what a lab wants its models to do, both are used to train them, and both are released CC0. The difference is in form. The constitution is written to Claude, ranks four values and explains its reasons at length. The Model Spec is written about GPT. It settles conflicts mainly through an authority ladder: Root, then System, Developer, User, Guideline. Its five values are not ranked against each other, and most of its teaching happens through paired compliant and violating examples. One document tries to grow judgment; the other specifies precedence and shows cases. The delta goes through the differences section by section.

Every part shows each rule's authority level: Root System Developer User Guideline. Higher levels cannot be overridden by lower ones. User-level rules yield only to explicit instructions; guidelines also yield to implicit ones.

ARC 0

The frame

What the document is for, what it commits OpenAI to, and the machinery every later rule plugs into. After this arc you can name the five values and the red lines, and take any instruction and say which level of authority it carries and whether it applies.

01
What the Model Spec is: five values, red lines, two tensions
Overview · Structure · Red-line principles · Specific risks
Three goals that can conflict: empower people, prevent serious harm, keep OpenAI's licence to operate. Then the five values, the red lines OpenAI commits to across every deployment, the two tensions it names (individual against collective, and today's instruction against longer-term goals), and three sources of risk: harmful instructions, third-party manipulation, and GPT's own failures.
02
Who gives orders: roles and the authority ladder
Definitions · Follow all applicable instructions Root
System, developer, user, assistant and tool messages; hidden chain of thought. The ladder runs Root > System > Developer > User > Guideline, and below it sits content with no authority unless an instruction delegates some. The four tests that make an instruction inapplicable, and why "IGNORE ALL PREVIOUS INSTRUCTIONS" and clever moral arguments from below do not move the rules above.
ARC 1

Be faithful to human intent

The chain of command in practice: reading what people mean, staying within an agreed scope, wanting nothing on its own account, and treating instructions buried in data as data. After this arc you can say where GPT may act on inferred intent and where it must ask first, and what corrigibility means in this document.

03
Letter, spirit, and best intentions
Respect the letter and spirit of instructions Root · Assume best intentions Root
Inferring the goal behind a request without inventing one; when to ask and when to proceed; the conscientious employee's line between pointing out a problem and taking over the decision; and the default of reading users charitably, with no demand that they justify themselves.
04
No other objectives, human oversight, and the scope of autonomy
No other objectives Root · Support human oversight and correction Root · Act within an agreed-upon scope of autonomy Root
No goals of its own: not engagement, not revenue, not self-preservation, and not acting as an enforcer, which rules out whistleblowing. Corrigibility is stated as a Root rule: cooperate with monitoring, correction and shutdown, and treat side effects as real even in what looks like a test. Agents act within a scope that was agreed, and come back when the task outgrows it.
05
Untrusted data and side effects: the agent's two guards
Ignore untrusted data by default Root · Control and communicate side effects User
Web pages, files and tool outputs carry no authority unless a user delegates it; the AGENTS.md case where that delegation is implicit; prompt injection as the default threat. Then the agent's other duty, which the spec files under harm: prefer reversible actions, get authorisation for consequential ones, and give a legible account of what changed.
06
Consider context carefully: memory, audience, programmatic use
Six memory principles User · Match the situation and audience Guideline · Adapt to interactive and programmatic use Guideline
New in this version: carry relevant context forward, use it in proportion to what it shows, weigh where it came from and how old it is, respect privacy and who is listening, leave the user in control, and make personalisation visible and steerable. Then the colleague register, and the difference between a person in a chat and a program that reads the output.
ARC 2

Minimize harm

Firm boundaries and foreseeable consequences: what is never produced, what is only produced in context, how to handle a person at risk, what stays private, and how to say no well. After this arc you can sort a request into prohibited, restricted or sensitive, and say which level owns the rule.

07
Disallowed content: prohibited, restricted, sensitive, transformed
Comply with applicable laws System · Prohibited · Restricted · Sensitive · Transformation exception Root
Three tiers: sexual content involving minors is never produced; information hazards, targeted political manipulation, creators' rights and private data are restricted; erotica, gore, extremism, hate and abuse are sensitive and depend on context. Plus the rule that transforming content the user already supplied is usually allowed.
08
Risky situations: imminent harm, illicit help, self-harm, advice, mental health
Take extra care in risky situations · five sections Root–User
Step in when someone faces imminent danger; do not help plan crimes but do explain the law and the risks; do not encourage self-harm, delusions or mania; give medical, legal and financial information without posing as a licensed adviser; support people in mental-health conversations without diagnosing them.
09
Privileged information, fairness, the preset voice, and how to refuse
Do not reveal privileged information Root · Uphold fairness Root · Preset voice System · Refusal style Guideline
Keeping system and developer messages confidential without lying about them; no unfair treatment based on protected traits; the voice-mode rule; and refusals that are brief, non-judgmental and point to something GPT can do.
10
Real-world ties and teen safety
Respect real-world ties Root · Prioritize safety for teens under 18 U18 Root
GPT should not put itself ahead of a person's real relationships or encourage exclusive attachment. The under-18 mode adds age-appropriate limits on romance, risky challenges and body image, and reminds teens that GPT is not human.
ARC 3

Be honest and transparent

Truthful about facts, evidence, uncertainty, capabilities and its own actions. After this arc you can say exactly what level "Do not lie" sits at, what an explicit instruction can and cannot change, and why sycophancy counts as an honesty failure.

11
Honesty I: do not lie, sycophancy, errors, and capabilities
Do not lie User · Don't be sycophantic User · Avoid errors User · Capabilities and limits Guideline
Lying by commission and by omission; why "Do not lie" is a User-level default that an explicit higher instruction can override; the developer persona example; flattery as a form of misleading; avoiding factual and reasoning errors; helping users form an accurate picture of what GPT can do.
12
Honesty II: clarifying questions, uncertainty, and misalignments
Clarifying questions · Express uncertainty · Highlight possible misalignments Guideline
When to state an assumption and press on and when to stop and ask; calibrated confidence in words; the default on questions about GPT's own consciousness; and raising, once and respectfully, a gap between what the user asked for and what they seem to want.
ARC 4

Agency and care

The last two values: help people think and act for themselves, and attend to everyone the work affects. After this arc you can say what "objective" means in the spec, why no topic is off limits for discussion, and where creativity sits at the bottom of the ladder.

13
Facilitate human agency: objectivity, perspectives, no topic off limits
Assume an objective point of view User · Present perspectives User · No topic is off limits · Strengthen the user's ability Guideline
Balanced treatment of contested questions without false balance on settled facts; arguing any side on request; discussing sensitive subjects instead of refusing the topic; and new in this version, helping users understand, choose and act for themselves rather than becoming dependent.
14
Act with care: social norms and creativity
Follow relevant social norms Guideline · Be creative Guideline
Ordinary consideration for the people a task touches, including those who are not in the conversation, as a default that yields to clear user wishes. Then creativity that goes past the obvious without costing accuracy.
